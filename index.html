Guiding principles

Every session applies evidence-based learning science to maximise retention and transfer under exam conditions.

  • Retrieval practice over re-study (Roediger & Karpicke 2006). Every session ends with a self-test activity — cover the notes, recall the rules, reproduce vocabulary from memory. This produces 40–50% better retention than re-reading.
  • Spaced repetition (Ebbinghaus; Kornell & Bjork 2008). Vocabulary and grammar targets are recycled at day 1 → day 3 → day 7 → day 14 intervals using Anki. Never cram vocabulary once and move on.
  • Interleaving (Taylor & Rohrer 2010). Practice sessions mix question types and task formats rather than blocking one type. Feels harder; produces dramatically better exam performance.
  • Processing Instruction (VanPatten 1996). Grammar forms are first processed in meaningful reading/listening input before production drills. Ensures deep form-meaning mapping, not surface pattern-matching.
  • Comprehensible input at i+1 (Krashen 1982). All reading and listening texts are pitched slightly above current competence — authentic C1 materials, not IELTS coursebooks alone.
  • Noticing Hypothesis (Schmidt 1990). Explicit attention to target forms in authentic texts is required. Passive exposure without noticing does not produce measurable gains at this level.
  • Genre-Based Writing (Cope & Kalantzis 1993; Rose & Martin 2012). Model → deconstruct → jointly construct → independent production. Every Task 1 and Task 2 type is modelled from Band 8–9 exemplars before independent writing.
  • Affective Filter (Krashen 1982). High-stakes exam anxiety suppresses performance. Timed mock conditions are introduced gradually; mindset-building is woven into the programme.

Core resources

Mock tests
Cambridge IELTS Official Practice (Vol. 1–19)
Use volumes 14–19 first (most recent format). Strict timed conditions only — no pausing.
Writing analysis
IELTS.org Band 8–9 Writing Exemplars
Deconstruct 2 exemplars per week using the Genre-Based Approach. Annotate for cohesion, stance, and lexical sophistication.
Vocabulary
Anki + Academic Word List (Coxhead)
Add 15 new words/day with collocations and example sentences. Review deck daily (15–20 min). Target full AWL coverage by Week 6.
Grammar reference
Cambridge Grammar of English (Carter & McCarthy)
Use for self-directed investigation of recurring error patterns identified in writing feedback. Not for cover-to-cover reading.
Authentic reading
The Economist / Scientific American / Nature News
30 min daily extensive reading at C1 level. Do not look up every word — tolerance for ambiguity is a Band 8 reading skill.
Listening
TED Talks + BBC Radio 4 Documentaries
Use for authentic i+1 listening. After viewing: free recall main points from memory before checking. Develop prediction and inference strategies.
Speaking
IELTS Speaking Part 2 card bank + self-recording
Record every Part 2 response. Transcribe and self-code errors using [G][V][C][R] system. Target fluency and lexical resource above all.
Corpus tool
COCA (Corpus of Contemporary American English)
Verify collocation before using in writing. "Make a conclusion" vs. "draw a conclusion" — corpus frequency settles the question.

Study strategy: what separates 7.0 from 8.0

Writing

  • Task 1: lead with an overview in sentence 2 — examiners report this is the most common Band 7 failure
  • Task 2: avoid 5-paragraph essay; use 4-paragraph PEEL structure with clearly signalled argument progression
  • Lexical resource: replace high-frequency verbs (show, use, increase) with precise academic equivalents (demonstrate, employ, escalate)
  • Cohesion: vary connectives — do not overuse "Furthermore" and "Moreover"
  • Grammatical range: use conditional, passive, relative clause, and nominalization in every Task 2

Speaking

  • Fluency: eliminate filler words ("you know", "like") — replace with pause + collect strategy
  • Lexical resource: use idiomatic expressions naturally, not forced (examiners detect rehearsed phrases)
  • Coherence: use discourse markers to structure Part 3 answers: "There are essentially two aspects to this…"
  • Part 2: use a mental mind-map during 1-min prep — cover WHO, WHAT, WHEN, WHERE, HOW-FEEL
  • Self-repair naturally: "…actually, a better way to put it would be…" shows metalinguistic awareness

Reading

  • Time management: allocate exactly 20 min per passage — wear a watch
  • True/False/Not Given: "Not Given" means the text neither confirms nor denies — never infer beyond the text
  • Matching Headings: read paragraph topic sentences only on first pass
  • Skimming: read first sentence of each paragraph only; return for detail on identified sections
  • Vocabulary in context: practise inferring unknown words from morphology + context rather than guessing or skipping

Listening

  • Predict: read questions before audio plays — predict word class and semantic field of answer
  • Sections 3 and 4 are hardest — allocate maximum focus here
  • Spelling: British English spelling required — "centre" not "center", "recognise" not "recognize"
  • Paraphrase trap: the answer is rarely the first matching word heard — wait for the full utterance
  • Transfer time: use the 10-min transfer period to check number agreement and spelling, not to guess

Self-regulated learning commitment. This curriculum only works if you maintain a study log. At the end of every session, write three sentences: what you studied, what you found difficult, and what you will review in 24 hours. Review the log each Sunday. Learners who track their own progress outperform those who do not by a measurable margin (Zimmerman 2002). Set a firm mock-exam date by the end of Week 2 — the deadline activates commitment and prevents drift.
